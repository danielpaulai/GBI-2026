## 3. The 7 Sales Beats — the spine of the talk

Every minute on stage maps to one of these. Run sheets in `DAY1_RUN.md` and `DAY2_RUN.md` are this skeleton in 1-minute resolution.

### Beat 1 — STATE FIRST (Day 1, 0–5 min)
Music in (entrance anthem). You enter walking. Don't open seated. First instruction: stand. Power breath × 5. Code of Honor agreement out loud. Pick your 3–5 barometer people in the first 90 seconds — different parts of the room, different demographics. They are your real-time feedback loop for the rest of the 4 hours.

### Beat 2 — IDENTITY POSITION (Day 1, 5–15 min)
Big Promise (Brunson). Origin story (Kiyosaki two-dad arc — your version: "I used to be the guy who… now I'm the guy who…"). Self-segmentation: "Hands up if you have ZERO AI employees. ONE. THREE. TEN+." The room sees the gap. The first declaration plants here: "Put your hand on your heart and say — I am the kind of person who builds systems that work for me." Repeat. *"Say it like you mean it."*

### Beat 3 — THE THREE BELIEF BREAKS (Day 1, 15–70 min)
Three "secrets" — each a story that breaks one objection bucket:
- **Secret 1 — Vehicle:** "AI doesn't work for [Asia / my industry / my size]." Break with a Singapore/Asia case study.
- **Secret 2 — Internal:** "I'm not technical." Break with the unlikely-builder story (older, non-coder, built one in 30 min).
- **Secret 3 — External:** "Even if it worked, I have no time/team." Break by showing the AI handling exactly that constraint.

Between each secret: pattern interrupts every ~12 minutes. Stand, partner-share, breath-of-fire, music swell, anchor word callback. **Never teach > 12 min in a flat block.** This is the fix for "you teach too much."

### Beat 4 — THE LIVE DEMO (Day 1, 70–85 min) + co-creation (Day 2, 35–70 min)
The single highest-leverage moment in the entire 4 hours.

- **Day 1 Demo #1 (Marketing AI, ~min 30):** volunteer pulled from the back. They press one button. Marketing campaign generates live in <60 seconds. Mission: Impossible theme plays. Three seconds of silence at the payoff. *"That's a week of work. In 45 seconds."*
- **Day 1 Demo #2 (Sales AI, ~min 65):** second volunteer + a "prospect" volunteer. Roleplay the AI's outreach. Audience laughs. *"I'd actually book a call after that message."*
- **Day 2 Demo #3 (Ops AI, ~min 35):** the showstopper. Three volunteers, three tools, AI ties them together. Full live dashboard. This is the one with the highest crash risk — pre-recorded backup queued one keystroke away.

Then on Day 2 (min 55–70), **co-create**: the audience designs their own AI employee on a worksheet, in pairs. They own the outcome before you sell.

Full choreography in `LIVE_DEMOS.md`.

### Beat 5 — DECLARATION & ANCHOR (Day 2, ~min 95)
Right before the stack. Eker-style:
> "Stand up. Hand on your heart. Say with me: 'I am the kind of person who builds systems that work for me.' Again. Louder. SAY IT LIKE YOU MEAN IT."

Fire your physical anchor (open hand to chest) for the third or fourth time in the talk. By now the gesture itself moves them. Hold the silence after. Then go quiet. Reflection bed (Hans Zimmer). This is the engineered valley right before the stack peak.

### Beat 6 — THE OFFER STACK + 3 CLOSES LAYERED (Day 2, 100–115 min)
Permission ask → future pace → stack slide build (each line drops on a beat, music swells) → if-anchoring (5 ifs) → price reveal pattern → emotional close → logical close → urgency close. Full script in `CLOSE_SCRIPT.md`.

### Beat 7 — JUST-ONE-THING CTA + RISK REVERSAL (Day 2, 115–120 min)
Brunson's "all I'm asking is one thing." Hormozi-grade risk reversal. Callback to Code of Honor. Final declaration → stand → walk to the back. Music peaks. Don't talk over the close. Let the room move.

## 4. The Show Layer — fixing "you teach too much"

June's note (not enough show, too much teach) is solved by inserting **one show element every 7–10 minutes**. There are 28 catalogued in `research/02-show-mechanics.md`. The high-frequency ones for this talk:

- **SE-6 Turn-to-Your-Neighbor (90s).** After every teaching frame. Use 3–5x per day minimum.
- **SE-7 Chorus Line.** Plant in first 10 min of Day 1: "Who builds the AI?" → "I DO!" Trigger every transition. By hour 4 it's tribal.
- **SE-15 Anchor word.** Plant a word ("Tuesday" / "the hard way") in first 15 min. Drop it as a comedy callback at every "old way" moment.
- **SE-3 Dance break.** ONE per session, deployed at the predicted slump (~min 58 Day 1, ~min 30 Day 2).
- **SE-13 Public declaration.** 2–3 per day. Standing-up, hand-on-heart Eker mechanic. By the close, the declaration is a reflex.

**The Streisand hook:** Mission: Impossible theme as the demo drop. 30-second clip plays before EVERY demo, every time, identical. By demo #3 the room cheers at the first four notes. They leave humming it. They hum it again Tuesday morning when they sit down to build their own. That's the trigger that makes them buy.

## 5. The Anchor Word + Catchphrase

Pick one of each in the first 15 minutes of Day 1. Rehearse them on every transition.

**Anchor word options** (pick one):
- **"Tuesday."** "Tuesday morning, you wake up, the AI has already done it." Drop every time you describe the post-cohort future.
- **"The hard way."** "We used to do it the hard way." Drop at every "old way" reference. Comedy callback: by hour 3, just saying it gets a laugh.

**Catchphrase options** (pick one):
- "Who builds the AI?" → "I DO!"
- "AI doesn't replace you. AI replaces…" → "the work you hate!"

Whichever you pick, plant it before minute 15 of Day 1 and use it 12+ times across both days. This is the "Barbra Streisand effect" you described — repetition is what makes it iconic.

## 6. Pre-stage routine (do this 60 min before the slot)

You cannot lead a state you're not in. Non-negotiable.

1. **2 minutes power breathing** (short fast nasal inhale, forceful mouth exhale, ~30 reps).
2. **Power pose** — V-shape arms, 60 seconds, alone in a green room or empty corner.
3. **Recite your opening 60 seconds out loud.** Not in your head. Out loud.
4. **Play your entrance anthem** (one earbud, low volume) while you walk to the wing. Same song every time.
5. **One physical anchor activation** — fire your "yes" gesture (open hand to chest) at peak energy. Lock the state in.
6. **Final ritual line** — one sentence you say to yourself at the curtain. Whatever yours is. Mine: "I'm here for them." Pick yours and use it every time.

## 8. Tech contingency principles

Three rules. Read these out loud once a day until May 14.

1. **Backup recording of every demo, queued in OBS or Keynote, ONE keystroke away.** AV operator's only job during demos is to be ready to cut. No exceptions.
2. **WiFi has cellular backup.** Personal hotspot, fully charged, as the failover. Test it Tuesday morning before the talk.
3. **Failure recovery scripts taped to the lectern.** Lines from `LIVE_DEMOS.md` § Recovery on a single index card. When something breaks, you read.

The Jerry Robert horror story you mentioned (his demo broke, his presentation almost screwed up) — that's a tooling failure, not a presenter failure. Steve Jobs at WWDC 2010 had WiFi die twice on stage. He stayed loose, made it a joke, and the audience loved him *more* after the failure. Your job is to stay in your body. The script does the rest.
