# 01 — Frameworks Research: Selling From Stage at GBI Singapore

**Purpose.** Extract the ACTUAL on-stage moves (not theory) that successful platform sellers use, and synthesize them into a single sales flow Danny can execute for his $1,497 AI-employee cohort close at GBI Singapore (90 min Day 1 + 120 min Day 2, Asia audience, ~50 Mandarin speakers).

**Reading note.** Where verbatim language is well-documented, I quote it. Where it isn't, I describe the move in plain language and flag it. Specifics on Cardone's exact courseware, Tony's UPW upsell mechanics, and Joel Bauer's 20-Move Matrix are paywalled — those sections describe moves rather than reproduce scripts.

## 1. Russell Brunson — Perfect Webinar / One Funnel Away

The single most-copied stage-selling structure in the modern info-product world. ~90 minutes mapped to: Intro (5) → Content (60) → Stack & Close (25). Brunson has publicly attributed ~$30M to this exact script.

### Core moves

- **The Big Promise (open, first 60 seconds).** "In the next 60 minutes, you're going to learn my exact strategy on how to [Result] without [Pain/Sacrifice]." Forces a clear frame.
- **The hook-to-end retention bait.** Tease a freebie/transcript/bonus that's only released at the END to live attendees ("You have to be watching live in order to get this, so stick around"). Pure attention insurance.
- **Command the room.** Ask the audience to close laptops, silence phones, move their bodies. Engineer compliance early so they're conditioned to act when you ask later.
- **Qualify yourself in story form, not bullets.** Origin story → struggle → discovery → result. Never read your résumé.
- **Future-pace.** Paint the post-event life of someone who actually applies what you teach. Make them see themselves there.
- **The "One Thing."** Force the entire teaching down to a single big idea. Everything else (the 3 Secrets) supports that one thing. Rule: if the audience can only repeat one sentence after the talk, what is it?
- **The 3 Secrets (the false-belief breaks).** Each "secret" attacks a specific objection bucket:
  - **Secret 1 — Vehicle.** "It's the wrong vehicle / this method doesn't work" → break with proof that THIS method (AI employees) works in THIS market.
  - **Secret 2 — Internal.** "I can't do it / I'm not technical / I'm not in the US" → break with story of someone unlikely (older, non-technical, Asia-based) who succeeded.
  - **Secret 3 — External.** "Even if I tried, [external block] would stop me" (no time, no team, my industry is different) → break with mechanism showing the external block doesn't apply.
  - Each secret = belief stated → story that contradicts it → mini-teach → "so the truth is…" landing.
- **Transition to offer (verbatim).** "Let me ask you a question…" Then ask permission: "Is it okay if I spend a few minutes going over a special offer I created to help you implement this?" The permission ask collapses sales resistance.
- **The Stack Slide.** A single slide listing every component of the offer cumulatively. Each new bonus is added to the slide (visually) one line at a time, with a price next to each. After every add, restate "and this alone is worth $X." End: "So the total real-world value is $XX,XXX." Brunson treats this as the highest-leverage part of the entire talk.
- **The "If all this did was…" if-anchoring.** Before the price reveal: "If all this did was [smallest believable result], would it be worth it? If all this did was [next result]…" Build five if-statements to lock in a yes.
- **Price drop pattern.** Show an inflated number first (often 2× actual), then drop. "I'm not going to charge you $1,997 — I'm not even going to charge you $997. Today, only for the people in this room, it's $1,497." Anchor high, drop with reason.
- **Just-One-Thing close.** "All I'm asking you to do is one thing — try it. If at any point you decide it's not for you, [risk reversal]." Reduces the decision down to a single low-friction yes.
- **The 3 Closes layered (Brunson's own framework, "316 The 3 Closes").**
  - 50% buy on **emotion** (status, identity, vision) — close with story.
  - 30% buy on **logic** (math, ROI, value-stack arithmetic) — close with the numbers.
  - 20% buy on **urgency/scarcity** (deadline, bonus expiration, seat cap) — close with a clock.
  Hit all three in sequence in the final 10 minutes. Don't pick one.
- **Risk reversal language.** "If you don't [get the result] in [timeframe], we'll [refund/keep working/give you the money + a bonus]." Hormozi's stronger version: a guarantee that's better than the offer itself.

### Verbatim language patterns

- "In the next [time], you're going to learn how to [outcome] without [pain]."
- "Let me ask you a question…"
- "Is it okay if I spend a few minutes going over a special offer I created to help you implement this?"
- "If all this did was [X], would it be worth it?"
- "I'm not going to charge you $X. I'm not even going to charge you $Y. Today, it's $Z."
- "All I'm asking you to do is one thing…"

### When to deploy

- **Open** — Big Promise, hook-to-end bait, command the room, origin story, future pace.
- **Build** — One Thing → 3 Secrets, each with belief-break + story + teach.
- **Close** — Permission ask → Stack → If-Anchoring → Price reveal → 3 Closes layered → Risk reversal → CTA with deadline.

## 2. Grant Cardone — 10X / Closer's Survival Guide

Cardone's gift to platform sellers is that he refuses to let the room exit on a "maybe." He treats hesitation as a stalling tactic to be dissolved, not respected.

### Core moves

- **The Close = 20% of selling time, 100% of income.** Cardone's central operating belief. Translation for stage: don't shrink the close. Spend real time there.
- **Treat "I need to think about it" as a known signal**, not a reason. Cardone teaches that "let me think about it" almost always means one of three things — the prospect can't say no, doesn't have full info, or doesn't have the money — and the closer's job is to surface which.
- **The 3-bucket reframe** (typical script): "When someone tells me they need to think about it, it usually means one of three things — either you're not sure this is for you, you don't have all the info, or it's a money issue. Which one is it?" Forces specificity.
- **Hard reframe on procrastination.** "You've already been thinking about this for a long time. More thinking won't fix it — only making a decision will." (From sales-training scripts attributed to this style.)
- **Cost-of-inaction frame.** "The only thing more expensive than a bad decision is making no decision at all." On stage: paint the 12-month cost of NOT buying — wasted hours, lost revenue, missed window.
- **Rather-have-a-no.** "If this isn't for you, I'd rather hear no right now — I won't be hurt. But don't tell me 'maybe' and walk out with no plan." Removes the social escape hatch.
- **What-would-it-take.** "What would I need to show you, or what would have to be true, for you to say yes today?" Surfaces the real objection.
- **Price-to-cost reframe.** Don't argue price — convert to daily cost vs. daily benefit. "$1,497 over a year is $4 a day. What's a single AI employee that does the work of a $4,000/month VA worth at $4 a day?"
- **Urgency without lying.** Cardone's urgency is anchored to real constraints: capacity, cohort dates, bonus expiration. Stage version for Danny: cohort starts June, only [X] seats, bonuses gone at [time].
- **126+ named closes in the Closer's Survival Guide.** The pattern across all of them: assume the sale, name the objection before they do, give them a structured way to say yes.

### Verbatim language patterns (sourced from sales-training adaptations of his style)

- "Whenever someone says 'I need to think about it,' it's one of three things…"
- "If this isn't for you, I'd rather have a no right now."
- "You've already been thinking about this. Thinking more won't fix it — making a decision will."
- "The only thing more costly than making a bad decision is not making one at all."
- "What would have to be true for you to say yes today?"

### When to deploy

- **Build** — plant the cost-of-inaction frame ("the cost of doing nothing is…").
- **Close** — when energy stalls, drop the 3-bucket reframe; if anyone is still on the fence after price reveal, hit "rather-have-a-no" + "what would have to be true."

## 3. Tony Robbins — UPW / Date With Destiny

Tony is the gold standard for state engineering at scale. Singapore audience, 50 Mandarin speakers, 4 hours total — Danny will lose them without state work.

### Core moves

- **Music as a state lever.** Tony walks the room with a literal DJ. Each section of his talk has a music cue: high-energy entrance, building intensity for breakthroughs, soft/emotional for vulnerable moments, anthem-rock for commitment. For Danny: pre-pick 4-5 tracks tied to specific beats (open, story peak, demo, close, exit).
- **Standing + movement = state shift.** Every 15-20 minutes Tony has the room stand up, jump, hug a stranger, change seats, or do a movement pattern. When energy dips, the FIRST move is physiological, not verbal.
- **Power breathing.** "Stand up. Breathe in fast — short, sharp — through the nose. Power breath in. Out. Faster." Used to interrupt sluggish state. Even 30 seconds resets the room.
- **Anchoring with hand gestures.** Tony deliberately repeats a fist-pump or open-palm gesture WHILE the audience is in a peak state (laughing, agreeing, cheering). Over the seminar, the gesture alone re-fires the state. For Danny: pick ONE physical anchor (e.g., open hand to chest, or fist to heart) and fire it every time the room hits a "yes" moment. By Day 2, the gesture itself moves them.
- **Pattern interrupts.** Random unexpected moves — sudden whispered sentence after loud delivery, a bizarre question, dropping to one knee, turning to silence — break the audience's predictive model and force re-attention. Use any time you sense drift.
- **Call-and-response baked in.** "Say yes!" / "Say YES!" / "Raise your hand if [X]." Builds compliance momentum — by the time you ask "raise your hand if you want this," they've raised their hand 40 times already.
- **Volunteer demo (the "intervention").** Tony pulls one person on stage, takes them through a real transformation (limiting belief → reframe → physical anchor → identity statement), and the audience experiences it through them. Danny's analog: pull one volunteer, show them building one AI employee live in 8 minutes. The whole room buys when one person visibly wins.
- **Future pacing.** "Imagine it's December, 12 months from now. You're sitting at your desk. Three AI employees are doing the work that used to take you 50 hours a week. What does that feel like? Where are you?" Engage senses — sight, sound, physical sensation. Don't rush this; let them sit in it for 30+ seconds.
- **Peak emotional anchor.** When the room is at its highest energy of the talk — ANCHOR. Music cue + gesture + verbal anchor ("This is who you are now"). That moment becomes recallable later in the close.

### Verbatim language patterns (general/well-documented)

- "Stand up. Right now. Stand up."
- "Say it like you mean it!"
- "Raise your hand if you understand what I'm saying."
- "Find someone next to you and tell them…"
- "Breathe in. Power breath. In through the nose."

### When to deploy

- **Open** — music + standing + power breathing in the first 90 seconds. Don't open seated.
- **Build** — pattern interrupts every 15 min; anchor peak yes moments; volunteer demo in the meaty middle.
- **Close** — future pace right before the offer reveal; fire the anchor at the moment of decision.

## 4. Robert Kiyosaki — Rich Dad / Cashflow Quadrant

Kiyosaki sells identity, not product. His stage skill is making the audience feel they're on the wrong side of an invisible line — and showing them the line.

### Core moves

- **The Two-Dad story.** Personal credibility through contrasted-character archetype: "I had two dads. One worked hard his whole life and died with nothing. One built systems and retired wealthy. They taught me opposite things." This single move bypasses 30 minutes of credentials.
- **The Cashflow Quadrant as identity diagnostic.** Draw the quadrant on stage (or one slide): E (Employee) / S (Self-employed) / B (Business owner) / I (Investor). Ask: "Where are you right now? Be honest. Raise your hand if you're an E. Now an S. Now a B. Now an I." Audience self-segments AND self-confronts.
- **The "system that works for you" frame.** "On the left side, you trade time for money. On the right side, you build a system that makes money whether you're awake or asleep." Danny's adaptation: "An AI employee is a system. Once you build it, it works while you sleep. That's the right side of the quadrant — and most of you have never built one. That's about to change."
- **Identity shift move.** Doesn't say "you should buy my course." Says: "What has to change isn't what you do. It's how you think." Then offers the path. People don't buy products; they buy a new identity.
- **Story-first, principle-second.** Every concept is delivered as a story (rich dad said X) with the principle dropped at the end. Never the other way around.
- **The board game / live tool demo.** Cashflow 101 the game is itself a sales tool — playing the game = experiencing the principle = buying the philosophy. Danny's analog: the live AI-employee build is the equivalent — they don't believe AI employees work until they SEE one work.

### Language patterns (well-documented from his books and talks)

- "I had two dads…"
- "The poor and middle class work for money. The rich have money work for them."
- "The single most powerful asset we all have is our mind."
- "An asset puts money in your pocket. A liability takes money out."

### When to deploy

- **Open** — open with a personal version of the two-dad arc ("I used to be the guy who…" → "now I'm the guy who…").
- **Build** — quadrant or equivalent diagnostic in the middle. Make the audience self-place. The discomfort is the move.
- **Close** — reframe the offer as crossing a quadrant line, not buying a course.

## 5. Blair Singer — Sales Dogs / Little Voice Mastery / Code of Honor

Singer's frameworks are particularly useful for an Asia audience because they let people self-identify into a "type" without losing face — and the Code of Honor move creates collective accountability that maps cleanly onto Asian collectivist culture.

### Core moves

- **The 5 Sales Dog breeds (audience self-recognition tool).**
  - **Pit Bull** — aggressive, persistent, hunts.
  - **Golden Retriever** — service-driven, relationship, loyalty.
  - **Poodle** — image, network, polished pitch.
  - **Chihuahua** — technical, intense, knows the product cold.
  - **Basset Hound** — patient, low-key, builds trust over time.
  - On stage: "Raise your hand if you're a Pit Bull. A Golden? A Poodle? A Chihuahua? A Basset?" Audience laughs, self-types, and now is BOUGHT IN to the rest of the talk because they're "in the conversation."
- **Little Voice management.** The 6 inches between your ears that says "you're not technical enough / you don't have time / this isn't for you." Singer teaches naming the voice, then over-riding it in 30 seconds. On stage: "Right now, your little voice is telling you something. What is it saying? Write it down." Then dismantle it during the talk.
- **Code of Honor (group commitment).** Get the room to agree out loud to a short list of rules ("We listen fully. We participate. We don't leave decisions to tomorrow."). Once stated and agreed, the room is now self-policing. By the close, you can call back the code: "You agreed at the start that you don't leave decisions to tomorrow. So…"
- **The hot-seat coaching demo.** Like Tony's volunteer move, but specifically for objection handling — pull one person up, surface their real objection live, walk them through breaking it. Whole room watches their own objection get broken without having to confess it.

### Language patterns

- "What's your sales dog breed?"
- "What's your little voice telling you right now?"
- "Are you willing to commit to a Code of Honor for the next two days?"

### When to deploy

- **Open Day 1** — Code of Honor in the first 10 minutes (this anchors the entire 4-hour arc).
- **Build** — Sales Dog typing as a self-recognition / engagement device.
- **Close** — call back the Code of Honor when objections come up.

## 6. T. Harv Eker — Millionaire Mind Intensive / SpeedWealth

Eker is the master of the cheap/free event → high-ticket close. His structural mechanic: turn every key idea into a physical-verbal declaration the audience MUST perform out loud, dozens of times, until the body believes the words. By the close, they're not buying a program — they're enrolling into the identity they've already been declaring.

### Core moves

- **The declaration formula.** "Put your hand on your heart and say…" → audience repeats the line aloud → "Touch your head and say 'I have a millionaire mind!'" → repeat. Used dozens of times across the 3-day event. Builds physical-verbal-emotional anchoring at industrial scale.
- **"Say it like you mean it!"** When the room mumbles, Eker stops and demands re-doing the declaration with full body. "I can't hear you. SAY IT LIKE YOU MEAN IT!" The room shouts. The energy spikes. He moves on. This is anchoring + state management combined.
- **The Money Blueprint reframe.** Core idea — your bank account today = the verbal/emotional programming you got about money before age 7. Reframe: "It's not your fault you're broke. It's your blueprint. And blueprints can be reset." Removes shame, opens buying state.
- **The 17 Wealth Files ("Rich people believe X. Poor people believe Y").** Each file is a binary identity choice:
  - "Rich people believe 'I create my life.' Poor people believe 'Life happens to me.'"
  - "Rich people play the money game to win. Poor people play not to lose."
  - "Rich people are committed to being rich. Poor people want to be rich."
  - "Rich people focus on opportunities. Poor people focus on obstacles."
  - "Rich people are willing to promote themselves and their value. Poor people think negatively about selling and promotion."
  - Each file → 3-min teach → declaration → "Touch your head, say 'I have a millionaire mind!'"
- **Standing ovations as compliance ratchet.** Eker engineers 5-10+ moments per day where the room spontaneously stands and cheers. By Day 3 they're physically conditioned to stand on cue. The close happens with the room standing.
- **The high-ticket close from a free event.** MMI is famously free or cheap. The close is for $5K-$15K Quantum Leap or other deeper programs. Mechanism: by Day 3, the audience has DECLARED so many identity statements (I am rich, I have a millionaire mind, I am committed) that NOT enrolling feels inconsistent with who they've been saying they are for 3 days. Identity-consistency closing.
- **"This is just the appetizer."** Explicit pre-frame: "What I'm sharing with you today is incredible — and it's just the appetizer. The full meal is in [program name], where we go 10 levels deeper and I work with you personally."

### Language patterns (verbatim, from his books and seminars)

- "Put your hand on your heart and say…"
- "Touch your head and say 'I have a millionaire mind!'"
- "Say it like you mean it!"
- "Rich people believe X. Poor people believe Y."
- "Your inner world creates your outer world."
- "It's not your fault. It's your blueprint."

### When to deploy

- **Open Day 1** — first declaration in the first 5 minutes. Set the precedent that this room speaks out loud and uses its body. Everything compounds from there.
- **Build** — pepper 2-3 declarations per teaching block. Every key principle gets declared.
- **Close** — final declaration is identity-locked to the offer ("Say: 'I am the kind of person who builds AI employees that work for me'") and the CTA follows immediately.

## 7. Master Facilitator / Train-the-Trainer / Making the Stage

The trainer-of-trainers world (Mike Pacchione, Joel Bauer, NLP Trainer's Trainings, Master Facilitator Program) shares a small set of high-leverage moves that rarely get talked about because they're invisible when done well.

### Core moves

- **Calibration (reading the room).** Pick 3-5 "barometer" people in different parts of the room within the first 5 minutes. Use them as your real-time feedback signal. If 2 of your barometers go quiet/distracted, the room is drifting — change something within 60 seconds (movement, story, question). NLP trainer's trainings explicitly teach this.
- **The energy curve.** Every long session has a natural slump 60-90 minutes in (in Asia, often after lunch on Day 2). Plan AGAINST it: schedule the highest-energy beat (volunteer demo, declaration, music spike) right at the predicted dip. Don't push through with content during a slump — change state.
- **Trainer state first.** You cannot lead a state you're not in. Pre-stage routine: 2 mins of power breathing + a power pose + reciting your own opening line aloud + a peak-state song. State leaks; bring the state you want the room to have.
- **Flow-state indicators in the room.** Watch for: laughter at unforced moments, audible "mmm" / agreement noises, leaning forward, taking notes without prompting, eye contact across the room (not just at you). When 3 of 5 are present, you're in flow — RIDE it. Don't break for a slide change.
- **The 3 anchors of facilitator presence.** Voice tonality (drop pitch on important lines, raise on questions) + open palms (never closed fists or pointing) + slow body movement (faster body = faster room = scattered attention; slow body = focused room).
- **Joel Bauer's "20-Move Matrix" mindset.** The thesis: a high-converting platform talk has a fixed sequence of psychological moves (intrigue → credibility → mechanism → proof → urgency → close), each with sub-techniques. The sequence matters more than the content. Bauer reportedly trained Belfort, Brunson, Polish, Pagan, Peng Joon. The exact 20 moves are paywalled, but the principle is: build the close into the structure from move 1.
- **The "future-you" close anchor.** End every major teaching block with "in 30 days from now…" or "by the time the cohort is done…" — let the room rehearse the future result before they buy it.

### Language patterns

- "Look around the room. See who's nodding. That's your tribe for the next two days."
- "I want you to do something with me right now…"
- "Notice what just happened. That's the principle in action."

### When to deploy

- **Pre-stage** — your own state work. Always.
- **Open** — pick your barometers within first 5 minutes.
- **Build** — manage the energy curve actively; never push content during a slump — change state first, content second.
- **Close** — voice/body/anchor congruence at the moment of CTA. The room reads incongruence as "this isn't important" and bails.

## SYNTHESIS — The 7 Sales Beats Danny Should Hit

A single combined sequence using the strongest moves from each framework, mapped to Danny's 90-min Day 1 + 60-min Day 2 build/teach + 60-min Day 2 close.

### Beat 1 — STATE FIRST (Day 1, minutes 0-5)
Music + standing + power breathing + Code of Honor agreement out loud.
- Tony's state engineering + Singer's Code of Honor + Eker's "say it like you mean it."
- The room should be on its feet, breathing hard, and verbally agreeing to a Code BEFORE you've said your name.
- Pick your 3-5 barometer people right here.

### Beat 2 — IDENTITY POSITION (Day 1, minutes 5-15)
Big Promise + your two-dad/origin story + the audience self-segments.
- Brunson's Big Promise: "In the next [X], you'll learn how to deploy an AI employee that does [outcome] without [pain]."
- Kiyosaki's two-dad arc, your version: "I used to be the guy who… now I'm the guy who…"
- Quadrant-style self-segmentation: "Raise your hand if you currently have ZERO AI employees. ONE. THREE. TEN+." Room self-segments, sees the gap.

### Beat 3 — THE THREE BELIEF BREAKS (Day 1, minutes 15-70)
Three "secrets," each a story that breaks one objection bucket.
- **Secret 1 (Vehicle):** "AI doesn't really work for [Asia/my industry/my size]" → broken by case study of a Singapore/Asia operator winning with this.
- **Secret 2 (Internal):** "I'm not technical enough" → broken by story of an unlikely person (older, non-coder) building one in 30 minutes.
- **Secret 3 (External):** "Even if I did this, I have no time/team" → broken by showing the AI employee handling exactly that constraint.
- Between secrets: pattern interrupts (Tony's move) — change of music, stand-up, partner-share. Every 15 minutes.

### Beat 4 — THE LIVE DEMO (Day 1, minutes 70-85 + Day 2 build phase)
Volunteer up. Build one AI employee live in 8 minutes on Day 1 (the "wow"). On Day 2's 60-min build/teach block, the room builds one with you.
- Tony's volunteer demo + Kiyosaki's board-game-as-tool principle: experiencing the principle = believing it.
- The whole room buys when one volunteer visibly wins on stage.
- This is the single highest-leverage 8 minutes of the entire 4 hours.

### Beat 5 — DECLARATION & ANCHOR (Day 2, transition into close, ~minute 60 of Day 2)
Eker-style declaration moment that locks identity to the offer.
- "Put your hand on your heart and say: 'I am the kind of person who builds systems that work for me.'" Repeat. "Say it like you mean it."
- Simultaneously fire your physical anchor (open hand to chest) for the third or fourth time in the talk. Audience now associates the gesture with this peak yes-state.
- Standing ovation if you've been engineering them; if not, get the room to physically stand for this declaration.

### Beat 6 — THE OFFER STACK + 3 CLOSES LAYERED (Day 2, ~25 min)
Brunson stack + Cardone reframes + Hormozi value equation, all in sequence.
1. **Permission:** "Let me ask you a question. Is it okay if I spend a few minutes on something I built specifically for the people in this room?"
2. **Future pace** (Tony's move): "Imagine it's December. You have three AI employees running. What does that feel like?"
3. **Stack slide build:** Add components one at a time. Each new line: "And this alone is worth $X." Total real-world value reveal: clearly more than $10K.
4. **If-anchoring (5 ifs):** "If all this did was save you 10 hours a week, would it be worth it? If all this did was let you fire one $4K/month VA…?"
5. **Price reveal pattern:** "I'm not going to charge you $4,997. I'm not even going to charge you $2,997. For the people in this room only, today, $1,497."
6. **Emotional close** (50%): the identity story — "This is who you are now."
7. **Logical close** (30%): the math — "$1,497 over 12 months is $4 a day. One AI employee replaces work worth thousands a month."
8. **Urgency close** (20%): the cohort starts in June, [N] seats, bonuses expire when you leave the room.
9. **Cardone reframes ready to go** for any push-back: "Whenever someone tells me they need to think about it, it's one of three things…"

### Beat 7 — THE QUANTUM-LEAP CTA + RISK REVERSAL (Day 2, last 10 min)
- Just-One-Thing close (Brunson): "All I'm asking is one thing — try it for the first weekend. If it's not for you, [risk reversal]."
- Risk reversal (Hormozi-grade): a guarantee better than the offer. E.g., "If you show up to all three weekends and you don't have at least one AI employee live and saving you 5+ hours a week, we'll refund you AND let you keep everything."
- Call back the Code of Honor: "Remember at the start of Day 1, we agreed not to leave decisions to tomorrow. The decision is right now."
- Final declaration: "Put your hand on your heart. Say: 'I'm in.' Everyone who is in, stand up and walk to the back of the room."
- Music swells. Don't talk over the close. Let the room move.

### Energy curve check (cross-cutting)
- **Predicted slumps for this format:** ~min 50 of Day 1, ~min 30 of Day 2 (post-lunch likely), ~min 75 of Day 2 (just before close).
- **Counter-moves at each:** standing + partner share (slump 1), live demo / hot seat (slump 2), declaration + standing ovation engineering (slump 3 — turn it into the launch ramp into the close).

Saying no to the offer becomes saying no to who they just told the room they are.
