# 02 — Show Mechanics for GBI Singapore (Day 1: 90 min / Day 2: 120 min)

A working playbook for Danny: how to insert "show elements" — recurring micro-beats (≤2 min) — into a Day 1 + Day 2 stage to keep energy compounding without losing teaching density. Built specifically to fix the "Day 2 gets boring → close dies" problem.

The core idea: stop teaching in flat blocks. Instead, design the set like a concert — verses, choruses, breaks, callbacks, drops — so the room is always either learning, laughing, moving, deciding, or being seen. Every 7–10 minutes something has to change.

---

## How to use this document

1. Print "The Show Element Bank" and tape it to the back wall of your tech booth. AV/MC reads from it.
2. Build your run-of-show by interleaving teaching blocks with at least one show element every 7–10 minutes. Mark them on your script as `[SE-3]`, `[SE-7]`, etc.
3. Each demo (Marketing AI / Sales AI / Ops AI) follows the same 4-beat anatomy in section 3.
4. The musical hook = your "Barbra Streisand Effect." Pick ONE signature track that triggers ONE recurring moment — repetition is what makes it iconic.

## 1. The Show Element Bank — 28 reusable beats

Tagging key:
- **E↑** = energy boost  | **E↓** = reflection / settle | **E=** = re-anchor / focus
- **Dur** = stage time
- **Cost** = how much it eats into teaching clock vs. how much state-shift you get

### A. Physical state-changers (use when the room feels heavy)

**SE-1 · Stand-up reset**
Dur 30s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
"Everyone on your feet. Stretch. Tap the person on your left, say 'I'm still here.' Sit down."
Use when: you've been teaching > 12 min. Best deployed mid-frame, not at slide breaks (unexpected = state shift).

**SE-2 · The Power Pose drop**
Dur 45s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
"Stand up. Both hands in the air, V-shape. Hold it for 15 seconds. Now drop your hands and write down the next thing I say." (Borrowed from UPW physiology work — posture shifts state in seconds.)
Use when: you're about to deliver a high-stakes idea and need them awake to receive it.

**SE-3 · 30-second dance break**
Dur 60s · E↑ · Cost: low
Cue music (see SE-22, "I Like to Move It" or "Don't Stop Me Now"). Stand up, no rules, 30 seconds. Lights flash. Music cuts. Sit. Resume.
Use when: post-lunch slump on Day 2 (this is the 2pm killer).

**SE-4 · "High-five three people"**
Dur 45s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
"Find three people you haven't met yet. High-five each one. Tell them what you just learned." Combines movement + retention + connection.

**SE-5 · Breath-of-fire reset**
Dur 30s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
"Hands on your knees. With me: in through the nose, hard out the mouth. Five times." (Brendon Burchard signature — fast nasal inhale, forceful exhale.)
Use when: you need them sharp, not happy. Pre-decision moment.

### B. Audience-as-co-creator beats (the antidote to "you teach too much")

**SE-6 · Turn-to-your-neighbor (TTN)**
Dur 90s · E= · Cost: medium
"Turn to the person next to you. Tell them in 30 seconds: what is the ONE thing you would automate first if you had an AI employee tomorrow morning?" Then: pull 2 answers from the floor.
Use when: after a teaching frame, before the next one. This is the staple. Use it 3–5x per day minimum.

**SE-7 · The Chorus Line**
Dur 30s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
A signature phrase you teach in the first 10 minutes of Day 1, then call back every time you finish a teaching point. Example pattern: You say "Who builds the AI?" — they shout "I DO!" Plant it on slide 3. Trigger it 12 times across 2 days.
Use when: every transition. By hour 4, the room owns the phrase. By Day 2 close, it's a tribal beat.

**SE-8 · The Show-of-Hands recognition**
Dur 60s · E= · Cost: low
"Raise your hand if you've spent more than $5,000 on a course that didn't change your business." [pause] "Look around. You are not alone. That stops today." Three-step pattern: question → pause → name what they're feeling.
Use when: you need them to feel seen before you sell. (Robbins, Eker, and Brunson all do this within the first 20 minutes.)

**SE-9 · Co-create the demo prompt**
Dur 2 min · E↑ · Cost: medium-high (worth it)
Before each AI demo: "I'm not picking the prompt. You are. Shout out: what's the most annoying task in your business right now?" Take 3 shouts, write them on the screen, let the room vote (loudest cheer wins). Then run the demo with THEIR prompt.
Use when: every live demo. Ownership = pre-sale.

**SE-10 · The Live Vote**
Dur 60s · E= · Cost: tiny
"Hands up: who would pay $1,000/month for an employee that does this 24/7?" Count visibly. "Who would pay $500?" Count. "Who would pay nothing because you'd build it yourself?" Count. The math sells itself.

### C. Recognition / "you are seen" patterns

**SE-11 · The Origin Acknowledgment**
Dur 90s · E↓ · Cost: low
"How many of you came here today knowing exactly what your business needs to look like in 12 months? [hands] How many of you came because something inside is screaming that the old way is dead? [hands] That second group — you're my people. I built this for you."
Use when: opening 10 minutes of Day 1. Plants the tribe.

**SE-12 · The Fear Naming**
Dur 90s · E= · Cost: low
"Here's what you're afraid of. You're afraid that AI will replace you before it works for you. You're afraid your competitor in Jakarta is already 6 months ahead. You're afraid you'll spend another year on the wrong thing." [silence]
Use when: just before the first big teaching frame on Day 1. (Eker's MMI pattern — name the limiting belief out loud, then dismantle.)

**SE-13 · The Public Declaration**
Dur 60s · E↑ · Cost: low
"Stand up if you commit to deploying ONE AI employee in your business in the next 30 days regardless of what you decide today. [pause for stand] Look around. These are your people for the next year." (Eker-style declaration; works because it's a low-stakes commitment that primes the high-stakes one later.)

**SE-14 · Spotlight a Story**
Dur 2 min · E= · Cost: medium
Pick someone whose hand you saw raised earlier. "You. Stand up. Tell us in 60 seconds why you're here." Listen. Repeat their key phrase back to the whole room. Connect it to the next teaching block.

### D. Callbacks & running gags (the "Streisand Effect" mechanic)

**SE-15 · The Anchor Word**
Dur 5s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
Pick a word in your first 15 minutes. Examples: "Manual." "Tuesday." "The hard way." Every time you reach a teaching moment about how things used to suck, drop the word. By hour 3, the room laughs the moment you say it. By Day 2, you can use it without a setup line.
The technique: comedy research (Duke, 500 Netflix specials) shows callbacks earn ~8 seconds of laughter vs. 3 for stand-alone jokes — because they reward attention.

**SE-16 · The Gesture Loop**
Dur 3s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
A physical move you do every time you launch a demo. Could be: pointing at the screen and saying "Watch this." Could be: rolling up sleeves. Whatever you pick, do it identically every time. By demo #3, the room cheers before you start.

**SE-17 · The Catchphrase Trigger**
Dur 5s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
A phrase you say. They complete. Example: You: "AI doesn't replace you. AI replaces…" Them: "the work you hate!" Plant on Day 1, hour 1. Trigger every transition for two days.

**SE-18 · The "Remember when I said…" callback**
Dur 30s · E= · Cost: tiny
At the close on Day 2: "Remember on Day 1, hour 1, when I said your competitor in Jakarta is already 6 months ahead? That's no longer a fear. That's now you." Closes the loop the audience didn't know was open.

### E. Music cues (these are the Streisand hooks)

**SE-19 · The Entrance Anthem**
Dur 60s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
Walk-on track. Same one every session, every break-back. The room learns it = your name. Recommendation: "Eye of the Tiger" is overused but works; consider "Lose Yourself" (Eminem), "Power" (Kanye), "Run This Town" (Jay-Z) — anything with a 4-bar build before vocals.

**SE-20 · The Demo Drop**
Dur 30s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
Same 30-second clip every time you launch a live demo. Recommendation: "Mission: Impossible" theme (5/4 time, instantly recognizable, signals "operation in progress"). The room hears those four notes = they sit up. By demo #3 they cheer at the first beat.

**SE-21 · The Stack Reveal**
Dur 45s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
When you stack value on the close. Recommendation: a slow-build cinematic track ("Time" by Hans Zimmer / "Now We Are Free" / "Heart of Courage" by Two Steps From Hell). Each item drops on a beat. The music keeps building. When the price reveals, the music peaks.

**SE-22 · The Movement Break**
Dur 60s · E↑ · Cost: low
Generic but works: "I Like to Move It," "Uptown Funk," "Don't Stop Me Now," "Dynamite." Used for SE-3.

**SE-23 · The Reflection Bed**
Dur 90s · E↓ · Cost: low
Soft underscore for journaling/visualization. Recommendation: "Comptine d'un autre été" (Yann Tiersen), "Experience" (Ludovico Einaudi), "Time" (Inception — quiet section).

**SE-24 · The Decision Moment**
Dur 30s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
The final pre-close moment, when you ask them to pick up the order form. Recommendation: a track with rising tension and no resolution yet. "In the Hall of the Mountain King" (build), "Stranger Things" theme, or low-bpm trap with a build. Cuts the second they pick up the pen.

**SE-25 · The Exit / Tribe Track**
Dur 90s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
Plays as people walk out at every break. Same song. Recommendation: a feel-good ensemble track — "Don't Stop Believin'" (Journey), "Ain't No Mountain High Enough," "September" (Earth, Wind & Fire). By Day 2 lunch they're singing along walking out the door. Tribal.

### F. Live demo show beats (covered in detail in section 3)

**SE-26 · The Volunteer Pull**
Dur 60s · E↑ · Cost: low
"I need someone who does NOT consider themselves technical. Stand up if that's you." Pick from the back of the room, not the front (visibility = shock value). Walk them up while the demo music plays.

**SE-27 · The "Wait, did it just…?" pause**
Dur 5s · E↑ · Cost: tiny
After the demo lands, freeze. Look at the screen. Look at the volunteer. Look at the room. Three full seconds of silence. The room fills it with applause every time.

**SE-28 · The Recovery Reframe (when something breaks)**
Dur 20s · E= · Cost: tiny
See section 5. Pre-scripted lines so when WiFi drops you don't panic.

## 2. Pacing curve template

Audience attention research (Throughline Group, 2Connect, Genard Method): adult attention drops sharply after 7–10 minutes of monologue. The fix is not less content; it's more *changes*. Every 7–10 minutes something must shift: speaker position, voice tone, audience action, music, or screen state.

**Energy Curve Principle (Heroic Public Speaking):** you cannot live in peak state. Peaks need valleys. The arc looks like waves, not a flat line. The close has to land on a peak — which means you must engineer a valley right before it so the rise feels earned.

## 3. Live demo choreography playbook

Every demo follows the same 4-beat anatomy. Apple, OpenAI DevDay, and Dreamforce all use variants of this. Steve Jobs reportedly rehearsed each demo for ~450 hours per 20-min keynote (per VerdanaBold / multiple Jobs analyses) — assume yours needs 10–15 full rehearsals minimum.

### The 4-beat demo anatomy

**Beat 1 — SETUP (60–90s)**
- SE-26 Volunteer Pull: "I need someone who does NOT consider themselves technical."
- SE-9 Co-create: "Tell me your most annoying task." (Take from a SECOND volunteer who stays seated — keeps two people invested.)
- Set the stake: "If this works, every person in this room can do this Tuesday morning. If it fails, I owe Jakarta an apology."

**Beat 2 — THE PRESS (30–60s)**
- Volunteer presses ONE button. That's it. Their job is theatrical, not technical.
- SE-20 Demo Drop music plays. Screen activates. Camera on the volunteer's face (have AV pre-set the cut — "VOLUNTEER CAM" should be a saved preset).
- You narrate, but light: "Watch the screen, not me."

**Beat 3 — THE PAYOFF (45s)**
- The AI does the thing. Output appears.
- SE-27 Pause: don't fill it. Look at the screen. Look at the volunteer. Look at the room. 3 full seconds of silence. Audience applauds.
- Payoff line: ONE sentence. Don't oversell.
  - Marketing AI: "That's a week of work. In 45 seconds. Done."
  - Sales AI: "That message would have taken your VA an hour. It just landed in the prospect's inbox."
  - Ops AI: "That report would cost you $400 from a contractor. It just cost you 12 cents."

**Beat 4 — THE ANCHOR (30s)**
- Thank the volunteer with stagecraft, not just words: physical handshake or hug, gift them a printed "I built an AI on stage" certificate or branded item, walk them back to seat with applause. This person now sells the cohort to their tablemates at lunch — their social proof is your asset.
- SE-15 Anchor word callback: tie the demo to your running gag.
- Transition line into next teaching block.

### Demo 1 — Marketing AI Employee (Day 1, ~min 30)

**Prompt menu** (audience picks one): "Write me a 7-day LinkedIn campaign for [their offer]" / "Give me 30 hooks for my next launch" / "Write a 12-email sequence for a $497 product."

**Volunteer instruction:** "When I say go, hit Enter. That's it." (Pre-loaded prompt with a placeholder for their answer.)

**Payoff visual:** split screen — old way (calendar with 7 hours blocked for "writing copy") vs. new way (full campaign in <60s).

**Recovery script:** if it stalls, see SE-28 / Section 5.

### Demo 2 — Sales AI Employee (Day 1, ~min 65)

**Prompt menu:** "Write me a personalized cold message to [LinkedIn URL audience picks]" / "Reply to this objection: '$1,497 is too expensive.'"

**Volunteer instruction:** Pull a SECOND volunteer to be "the prospect." Have them read the AI's message out loud to the first volunteer like a roleplay.

**Payoff visual:** Show the AI's response side-by-side with a generic templated message. The room sees the gap.

**Sticky moment:** at the end, the volunteer says: "I'd actually book a call after that message." Audience laughs. That's the moment.

### Demo 3 — Ops AI Employee (Day 2, ~min 35) — the showstopper

**Setup:** This is the demo you spend the most rehearsal on. It's also the most likely to fail. Build a hardcoded fallback (see Section 5).

**Prompt menu:** "Pull last 30 days of [Stripe / Gmail / a connected tool] and tell me the 3 things I should focus on this week."

**Volunteer instruction:** Pull THREE volunteers — make it a "team." Each presses one button on a different tool. The AI ties them together.

**Payoff visual:** A live dashboard or report appears on screen. Pre-recorded as backup — the room doesn't know if it's live or backup. Doesn't matter.

**Anchor moment:** "This is the difference between owning a business and being owned by one. Tuesday morning, that report is in your inbox before you have coffee." [SE-23 reflection music kicks in for 30 seconds]. Roll into the close arc.

## 5. Failure recovery scripts

The cardinal rule: assume your demo will fail. Steve Jobs at WWDC 2010 had Wi-Fi die on stage — twice — and recovered by joking "I've got time" and asking the audience to turn off their hotspots. The audience loved him MORE after the failure than before, because he stayed in his frame. (InfoWorld / Cult of Mac coverage of Jobs WWDC 2010 meltdown.)

Three rules before any of these scripts:
1. **Have a screen-recorded backup of every demo, queued in OBS or Keynote, ONE keystroke away.** AV operator's only job during demos is to be ready to cut to it.
2. **Never apologize.** Apologizing tells the room something is broken. Reframe.
3. **Stay in your body.** The room reads your physiology before they read the screen. If you stay loose, they stay loose.

### Recovery scripts (write these on a card, tape to the lectern)

**A. WiFi dies / API timeout mid-demo**
> "And there's the moment every founder knows — the moment your tech betrays you in front of 200 people. Welcome to my Tuesday. [pause for laugh] Watch this — [cut to backup recording]. This is what it looked like 30 minutes ago in the green room. Same prompt, same outcome."
> Why this works: you've named the failure, made yourself the underdog, and pivoted to the backup as if it were always the plan.

**B. Volunteer freezes / can't think of a prompt**
> "And THIS is exactly why we built this. You're standing in front of 200 people and your brain just deleted everything you've ever wanted to do. So I'll do you a favor — I'll give you mine. [hand them prompt]. Press the button."
> Why this works: you absorbed their failure as your gift. Audience now likes them more.

**C. AI generates something weird / wrong / NSFW-adjacent**
> "And this is the second thing we teach in the cohort: how to manage what an AI says BEFORE it says it. Because right now, this thing has no idea what your brand sounds like. Day 6 of the cohort, this never happens again. [advance slide]"
> Why this works: you've turned a failure into a curriculum hook. Bug → feature.

**D. Joke flops / audience is silent**
> "Tough crowd. [pause] Let me try that again on Day 2 and we'll see if it lands. [continue]"
> Why this works: improv "yes-and" — accept the silence, move forward, don't double down on a dead joke. The audience respects you for not begging.

**E. You lose your place / forget what you were going to say**
> "I just had a moment where I forgot what I was going to say next, and it's a perfect example of why your AI employee shouldn't be inside your head. [pause for laugh] Where were we — somebody help me out, what was I just talking about?"
> Why this works: you've made forgetting the proof point. Audience now invested in helping you.

**F. The whole tech stack is down (worst case)**
> "We are going to do this old-school. Everybody close your laptops. I'm going to teach you the framework that makes the tech work, and when it comes back up, you'll understand why it does what it does. The framework is more important than the demo anyway."
> Why this works: you've made the failure prove your thesis ("the framework matters more than the tools"). This is the move you keep in your back pocket and pray you never need.

**G. Mid-pitch heckler / objection from the floor**
> "I love that you said that out loud. Half the room is thinking it. [address it directly, briefly] And here's why I still think it's worth it for the people in this room — [deliver the answer]. Anyone else got the question they're afraid to ask?"
> Why this works: heckler becomes voice-of-the-room. You've defused them by validating, not fighting.
