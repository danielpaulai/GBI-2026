# LIVE DEMOS — full choreography

Three demos. Same 4-beat anatomy each. Rehearse 10–15 times each before the event. Steve Jobs reportedly rehearsed each demo for ~450 hours per 20-minute keynote — the demo carries more weight than any other 8 minutes of stage time, so the rehearsal hours are non-negotiable.

**The 4-beat anatomy** (use for all 3 demos):
1. **SETUP** (60–90s) — volunteer pull, co-create the prompt, set the stake.
2. **THE PRESS** (30–60s) — volunteer presses ONE button. Music drops. Camera on volunteer.
3. **THE PAYOFF** (45s) — output appears. 3-second silence. ONE payoff line.
4. **THE ANCHOR** (30s) — volunteer back to seat with stagecraft, anchor word callback, transition.

**The cardinal rule:** every demo has a screen-recorded backup, queued in OBS or Keynote, ONE keystroke away. AV operator's only job during demos is to be ready to cut to it. No exceptions.

# DEMO 1 — Marketing AI Employee
**Day 1 · ~minute 17 · Block C · the first WOW**

## Pre-stage prep
- AI tool preloaded in browser, full-screen, login confirmed. Test the prompt 30 min before stage.
- Backup recording (60s screen capture) of the same prompt running successfully — queued in OBS, hotkey ready.
- Volunteer mic (handheld) charged and ready, off-stage.
- AV preset: "VOLUNTEER CAM" — close-up shot of volunteer's face when they press the button.
- Mission: Impossible theme — 30s clip — queued.
- Slide deck: split-screen "old way / new way" template ready.

## Beat 1 — SETUP (60–90s)

> **"I need someone in this room who does NOT consider themselves technical. Stand up if that's you."**

[Wait. People stand. Pick from the BACK of the room — not the front. Visibility / shock value matters. Pick someone who looks like the audience's median, not someone who looks pre-coached.]

> **"You. Yes, you. Come on up."**

[Walk them up while Mission: Impossible theme starts (low). Hand them the volunteer mic. Quick warm-up: "What's your name? What do you do?" 20 seconds of them on mic before any pressure.]

> **"Now — I'm not picking the prompt. The room is. Audience: shout out the most annoying marketing task in your business right now. The thing you keep putting off. Three shouts."**

[Take 3 shouts. Repeat each one back. Let the room vote with cheers — loudest cheer wins. Put the winner on the screen as the prompt input.]

> **"Set the stake. If this works, every person in this room can do this Tuesday morning. If it fails, I owe Jakarta an apology."**

[Light laugh. Tension up.]

## Beat 2 — THE PRESS (30–60s)

> **"[Volunteer name] — when I say go, you press ONE button. That's it. Your job is theatrical, not technical."**

[Mission: Impossible theme up. Volume up. Camera cuts to VOLUNTEER CAM.]

> **"GO."**

[Volunteer presses. Output starts streaming on screen. You step back. Don't narrate. Let the screen do the work.]

## Beat 3 — THE PAYOFF (45s)

[Output finishes. AI has generated a 7-day marketing campaign. ~60 seconds of total runtime.]

[**SE-27 — THREE FULL SECONDS OF SILENCE.** Look at the screen. Look at the volunteer. Look at the room. Don't move. Don't breathe. The room fills the silence with applause.]

> **"That's a week of work. In 45 seconds. Done."**

[Hold. Let it land.]

## Beat 4 — THE ANCHOR (30s)

> **"[Volunteer name], you just built a Marketing AI Employee on stage in front of 300 people. That's now true forever."**

[Handshake. Or — better — give them a small physical gift: a printed "I built an AI on stage" certificate, or a branded item. They walk back to seat with applause. They are now your evangelist at the lunch table.]

> **"Look at what just happened. One person. One button. One prompt. A week of marketing work — gone."**

[Anchor word callback. Catchphrase trigger. Transition to teach block.]

> **"Who builds the AI?"** — *"I DO!"*

## Slide cue
1. Volunteer cam preset (full screen of volunteer's face during the press).
2. Split-screen output (old way: calendar with 7 hrs blocked vs. new way: campaign in <60s).
3. Anchor word slide for the callback.

## Failure recovery (specific to Demo 1)

**A. Prompt produces nothing / API timeout.**
> "And there's the moment every founder knows — the moment your tech betrays you in front of 300 people. Welcome to my Tuesday. [pause for laugh] Watch this — [AV cuts to backup]. This is the same prompt run 30 minutes ago in the green room. Same outcome."

**B. Volunteer freezes / can't think of how to press.**
> "Right here. This button. One press. [you point. Volunteer presses. Move on.]"

**C. AI generates something weird or off-brand.**
> "And THIS is the second thing we teach in the cohort — how to manage what an AI says BEFORE it says it. Day 6 of the cohort, this never happens again. [advance slide]"

# DEMO 2 — Sales AI Employee
**Day 1 · ~minute 45 · Block E · the sticky moment**

## Pre-stage prep
- AI tool preloaded with cold-message generator, full-screen.
- Backup recording (60s) queued one keystroke away.
- TWO volunteer mics ready.
- Slide ready: AI message side-by-side with a generic templated message (post-demo reveal).
- Mission: Impossible theme queued.

## Beat 1 — SETUP (60–90s)

> **"Different person this time. I need someone who has TRIED — and given up — on cold outreach. Stand up if that's you."**

[Pull from a different part of the room than Demo 1. Walk them up.]

> **"And — I need a SECOND volunteer. Stay seated. You're going to be 'the prospect.' I'll send you the AI's message and you read it out loud like you just got it in your inbox."**

[Pull second volunteer from a different table. Hand them a mic. Two volunteers active now — first standing on stage, second seated in audience. Both visible.]

> **"Audience — whose LinkedIn URL should we send to? Three shouts. Loudest cheer wins."**

[Take 3 shouts. Pick winner. Put on screen.]

> **"Volunteer one — press the button when I say go. Volunteer two — when the message appears, you read it out loud, in character, as if you just got this in your inbox at 9:00 a.m. Tuesday morning."**

## Beat 2 — THE PRESS (30–60s)

[Mission: Impossible theme up.]

> **"GO."**

[Volunteer 1 presses. AI generates a personalized cold message in ~20 seconds. AV cut to the message on screen.]

## Beat 3 — THE PAYOFF (45s)

> **"Volunteer two — read it. Out loud. Like you just got it."**

[Volunteer 2 reads the AI's message in character. The room hears it as if they're the prospect. This is the magic of this demo — they're not seeing the AI work; they're FEELING what it's like to receive its work.]

[**SE-27 — THREE SECONDS OF SILENCE.**]

> **"Volunteer two — be honest. Would you reply to that?"**

[The volunteer (you've coached them slightly during the setup, OR it's organic) says: **"I'd actually book a call after that message."** Audience laughs. The room buys.]

## Beat 4 — THE ANCHOR (30s)

[Side-by-side slide: AI message vs. a generic templated cold message you've pre-loaded. The room sees the GAP.]

> **"That message just took 20 seconds. The generic version on the right took your VA an hour. Both will land in someone's inbox tomorrow morning. Only one will get a reply."**

[Anchor both volunteers back to seat with applause.]

> **"Who builds the AI?"** — *"I DO!"*

## Slide cue
1. Volunteer cam (volunteer 1 pressing).
2. Audience cam OR full-screen of the AI message as it generates.
3. Side-by-side post-reveal: AI message vs. generic.

## Failure recovery (specific to Demo 2)

**A. Volunteer 2 says "no I wouldn't reply" — KILLS the moment.**
This is the highest-risk failure because you can't predict it. **Coach volunteer 2 in advance** — when you pull them up, lean in and quietly say: "Read it like you'd actually be intrigued. The room is going to laugh — that's the moment." Most people, when given that prompt, deliver. If volunteer 2 still says no, recovery:
> "Fair. Honest answer. So let's break down WHY — what would have made it a yes? [take their answer, turn it into the next teach block]. THAT — that thing they just said — that's exactly what the cohort teaches you to put into the message. We just turned a no into a curriculum."

**B. AI generates something weak.**
> "And this is real cohort gold — let's edit it together. Audience, what would make this message land harder? [take 2 inputs, edit live]. THAT'S the cohort. Not the AI alone — the AI plus your judgment. The cohort makes you the editor."

# DEMO 3 — Ops AI Employee (THE SHOWSTOPPER)
**Day 2 · ~minute 33 · Block C · highest crash risk, highest payoff**

## Pre-stage prep — most rigorous
- This demo is the most ambitious. Build a hardcoded fallback path. The audience won't know if it's live or backup. Doesn't matter.
- Three "tool" stations on stage (or three buttons on the same screen — your call).
- Live dashboard or report layout pre-tested. Run the full demo end-to-end at least 5 times in the venue WiFi the morning of the talk.
- Cellular hotspot on standby.
- Backup recording (90s) queued one keystroke away. **AV operator: this is your single most important job for the entire 4 hours.**
- Three volunteer mics charged.
- Reflection bed (SE-23 — Hans Zimmer "Time" or similar) queued for the anchor moment.

## Beat 1 — SETUP (90s — slightly longer because it's a 3-volunteer demo)

> **"Yesterday I built marketing and sales. Today we build the OS — the Operations AI. The one that pulls everything together and tells you, every Tuesday morning, what to actually focus on this week."**

[Anchor word land.]

> **"For this one — I need THREE volunteers. One who runs sales. One who runs marketing. One who runs ops. Stand up — yes, you, you, and you."**

[Pull three volunteers from three different parts of the room. Walk them up. Hand mics to all three.]

> **"Each of you is going to be one of three AI inputs. Sales person — your job is to press THIS button [point]. Marketing person — THIS one. Ops person — THIS one. The AI is going to pull data from all three of you and tell us — the room — the three things this business should focus on this week."**

> **"Audience — the BUSINESS we're running today is mine. Real Stripe data, real Gmail, real workflow. We're doing this on me. So if it's a disaster, you'll see it. If it works, you'll see it."**

[Stake set. Vulnerability is a weapon here — you're putting your real business on the screen.]

## Beat 2 — THE PRESS (45s)

[Mission: Impossible theme up.]

> **"GO. In sequence — sales, marketing, ops."**

[Volunteer 1 presses. Then volunteer 2. Then volunteer 3. Each press triggers a small visual confirmation on screen (e.g., a light goes green). Music builds.]

[All three are now "loaded." The AI runs. ~15 seconds of compute. AV cut to the live dashboard.]

## Beat 3 — THE PAYOFF (45s)

[Live report appears on screen. Three things to focus on this week, with a one-line rationale each.]

[**SE-27 — THREE FULL SECONDS OF SILENCE.** This silence carries the whole talk.]

> **"That report — exactly that report — would cost you $400 a month from a contractor. It just cost you 12 cents. And it took 18 seconds."**

[Pause. Let the math land.]

## Beat 4 — THE ANCHOR (30s — the most important anchor in the whole 4 hours)

> **"This is the difference between owning a business and being owned by one."**

[SE-23 reflection bed kicks in. Quiet. Music underneath.]

> **"Tuesday morning. June 30. You sit down with coffee. This report is in your inbox. You don't WONDER what to focus on. You don't STARE at your inbox. You don't have that 9:00 a.m. paralysis where you can't pick a task because there are 47 of them. The AI already picked the three. You execute. Done by lunch."**

[Hold for 5 seconds. Reflection bed underneath.]

> **"That is the cohort. Three weekends from now, this is your Tuesday."**

[Anchor word callback. Three volunteers back to seat with group applause. Mic out. Stagecraft.]

> **"Who builds the AI?"** — *"I DO!"*

## Slide cue
1. Three-station diagram (which volunteer presses what).
2. Volunteer cams (multi-cam if AV supports — three faces at once).
3. Live dashboard (or backup recording).
4. Reflection slide for the anchor moment ("Tuesday morning, June 30...").

## Failure recovery (specific to Demo 3)

**A. WiFi dies / API timeout (most likely failure).**
> "And there's the moment every founder knows — the moment your tech betrays you in front of the people you most want to impress. Welcome to my actual Tuesday. [pause for laugh] Watch this — [AV cuts to backup]. This is the same dashboard, same data, run 30 minutes ago. Same outcome. The future is just slightly delayed."

**B. One of three volunteers freezes.**
> "Hold on. [walk over]. Let me press it with you. [press together]. There — see? You're the operator. You don't have to be the tech. That's the whole cohort right there."

**C. AI generates a wrong / weird recommendation.**
> "And THIS — exactly this — is why the cohort teaches the SECOND principle: AI without judgment is a faster bad employee. The cohort makes you the manager. Watch — [edit the recommendation live]. That's the move."

**D. Whole tech stack is down (worst case).**
> "We're going to do this old-school. Everybody — close your laptops. I'm going to teach you the FRAMEWORK that makes this report work. Three integrations, one decision layer. The framework matters more than the tool. When your tech comes back up tonight in your hotel, you'll know exactly what you're looking at. [continue with whiteboard / verbal teach]."

This last one is the move you keep in your back pocket and pray you never need. But it's the move that turns a disaster into a "the framework is what matters" curriculum hook.

# Universal demo recovery card (tape to lectern)

| Failure | Line |
|---|---|
| WiFi / API timeout | *"That's the moment every founder knows. Welcome to my Tuesday. Watch this — [cut to backup]."* |
| Volunteer freezes | *"Right here. This button. One press."* (Press it with them.) |
| AI generates weird output | *"That's the second thing we teach in the cohort — managing what AI says before it says it. Day 6, this never happens."* |
| Joke flops | *"Tough crowd. Let me try again on Day 2."* (Move on. Don't double down.) |
| You forget your place | *"I just had a moment where I forgot what I was going to say next — perfect example of why your AI shouldn't be inside your head. Somebody help — what was I just talking about?"* |
| Whole stack down | *"We're going old-school. Everyone close your laptops. I'm going to teach you the framework — the framework matters more than the tool."* |
| Heckler / objection from floor | *"I love that you said that out loud. Half the room is thinking it. [validate, briefly answer, move]. Anyone else got the question they're afraid to ask?"* |

# Volunteer coaching (for ALL demos)

When you pull a volunteer up, you have ~20 seconds with them off-mic before the demo starts. Use it.

**Standard coaching:**
1. *"What's your name? What do you do?"* (Warmup. Calms them.)
2. *"This is going to take 90 seconds. You press one button. That's your whole job."*
3. *"If something feels weird — look at me. I've got you."* (Permission.)
4. *"At the end the room is going to applaud. Just enjoy it."*

**For Demo 2 specifically (volunteer 2):**
- *"When the message appears, read it like you'd be intrigued. The room is going to laugh. That's the moment."*

**For Demo 3 specifically (three volunteers):**
- *"You three are a team for the next 90 seconds. When I say go — sales first, then marketing, then ops. One press each. You don't even have to look at the screen. Look at the room."*

The volunteer's confidence is your confidence. If they're rattled, the demo dies. If they're loose, the demo lands. Spend the 20 seconds.
