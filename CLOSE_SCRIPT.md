# CLOSE SCRIPT — Day 2, minutes 98–120

The 22-minute sales close. Permission ask → future pace → stack → if-anchoring → price reveal → 3 closes layered → CTA + risk reversal → callbacks → final declaration.

Memorize the spine (the bolded lines below). The connective tissue can flex; the spine cannot.

---

## Pre-frame (the engineered valley before this — minutes 88–98)

You've just told your vulnerable story. Future-paced them to December. They're settled, quiet, leaning forward. Music has faded. You're standing again.

**Do not rush. The value of what you're about to do is set by the silence before you do it.**

---

## SECTION 1 — Permission ask + pre-frame · 98–100 min

> **"Let me ask you a question."**

[Pause 3 seconds.]

> **"Is it okay if I spend a few minutes going over something I built specifically for the people in this room?"**

[Wait. They will nod. Wait until you can see the nodding before continuing.]

> "Because here's what I know. Some of you are going to walk out of this room tonight and try to do this on your own. And I respect that. Some of you are going to forget about it by Friday. And some of you — some of you — are going to want a path. A path with me, with a group of people who are doing this with you, with the templates and the system already built. That's what this is."

> "I'm going to walk you through it. Then I'm going to give you a chance to choose."

## SECTION 3 — If-anchoring · 108–110 min

The 5 ifs. Spoken slowly. Eye contact across the room.

> **"If all this did was let you fire one $4,000-a-month VA, would it be worth it?"**

[Pause. Some nods.]

> **"If all this did was give you back 10 hours a week — 40 hours a month — 480 hours a year — would it be worth it?"**

[Pause.]

> **"If all this did was let you sleep on Sundays again because the marketing campaign's already done, would it be worth it?"**

[Pause.]

> **"If all this did was prove to you — finally — that you CAN build something — would it be worth it?"**

[Pause.]

> **"If all this did was put you in a room with 100 other founders who are doing this WITH you, so you're never doing it alone — would it be worth it?"**

[Long pause. Hold the room.]

## SECTION 4 — Price reveal · 110–112 min

> **"So here's the question. What's it worth?"**

[Pause.]

> **"$15,488 is what it's worth. But that's not what you're paying."**

> **"I'm not going to charge you $4,997."**

[Pause.]

> **"I'm not going to charge you $2,997."**

[Pause.]

> **"For the people in this room — today — $1,997."**

[Slide: **$1,997**. Music PEAKS on the number. Music holds, doesn't fade.]

[**HOLD THE NUMBER ON SLIDE FOR 5 FULL SECONDS WITHOUT TALKING.** This is the most important silence of the entire 4 hours.]

## SECTION 5 — 3 closes layered · 112–115 min

### Emotional close (50% buy here)

> **"This isn't about $1,997. This is about who you are now."**

> **"For the last two days you've been telling this room — telling yourselves — that you're the kind of person who builds systems that work for them. You said it with your hand on your heart. You said it standing up. You said it like you meant it."**

> **"This is the moment that's true."**

---

### Logical close (30% buy here)

> **"Let's talk math. $1,997 over 12 months is four dollars a day. Four dollars."**

> **"You spend more on a coffee."**

> **"Now compare. ONE Marketing AI replaces a $1,500-a-month freelance copywriter. ONE Sales AI replaces a $4,000-a-month VA. ONE Ops AI saves you the cost of a $400-a-month reporting consultant."**

> **"You're paying $1,997 once. You're getting back five-figures of work every month. Forever."**

---

### Urgency close (20% buy here)

> **"And here's the timing. The pre-bootcamp starts June 12. That's 27 days from right now."**

> **"The cohort caps at [N] seats. After this room, the price is $2,497 — no Founder OS. The Founder OS access is room-only. You walk out without buying, you cannot get this stack again."**

> **"This is now. Or it's never at this price."**

---

## SECTION 6 — Just-One-Thing close + risk reversal · 115–117 min

> **"All I'm asking you to do is one thing."**

> **"Try it. Show up to the pre-bootcamp on June 12. Two days. If by the end of June 13 you don't think this is the most valuable two days of your year — full refund. AND — you keep everything I've given you so far."**

> **"You can't lose. The only way to lose is to not show up."**

---

## SECTION 7 — Callbacks · 117–118 min

> **"Day 1, hour 1 — I told you your competitor in Jakarta is already 6 months ahead. Remember that?"**

[Pause.]

> **"That's no longer a fear. As of this moment — that's no longer you."**

> **"And Day 1, hour 1 — we agreed to a Code of Honor. Three things. Listen fully. Participate fully. Don't leave decisions to tomorrow."**

> **"The decision is right now."**

---

## SECTION 8 — Final declaration + CTA · 118–120 min

[Music: SE-24 Decision Moment in. Building tension.]

> **"On your feet. Right now. Stand up."**

[Wait for them to stand.]

> **"Hand on your heart."**

> **"Say with me — 'I'M IN.'"**

[They say it.]

> **"Louder. SAY IT LIKE YOU MEAN IT. 'I'M IN.'"**

[They say it. The room is up. Music swells.]

> **"Everyone who is in — walk to the back of the room. Right now. Order forms are there. Crew is there. Founder OS access starts tonight."**

[**Stop talking. Step back from the mic. Watch the room move.**]

[Catch eyes with each of your barometer people. Nod at them. They give permission to the rows around them.]

> **(Quiet, almost reverent.) "Who builds the AI?"**

[Audience: **"I DO!"**]

[Walk off. SE-25 Exit / Tribe track in. Mac is up next.]

# OBJECTION BANK — what to say if anyone holds you up

These are the responses to the 12 most common objections you'll hear during and after the close. The crew uses these too — abbreviated versions in `CREW_BRIEF.md`.

---

### O-1. "I need to think about it."

> **"I get that. Let me ask you something — when someone tells me they need to think about it, in my experience it usually means one of three things. Either you're not sure this is for you. Or you don't have all the information you need. Or it's a money issue. Which one is it for you?"**

(Cardone's 3-bucket reframe. Forces specificity. Once you know which bucket, you address the actual concern, not the smokescreen.)

---

### O-2. "I need to talk to my [spouse / business partner]."

> "Of course. Here's what I'd say — what would you need to be able to tell them, in one sentence, that would make this a yes? Tell me, and I'll give you the line."

(Reframe: you're helping them sell to the spouse, not asking them to defend it alone.)

> "And the risk reversal exists for exactly this situation. If you talk to them tomorrow and they say no — full refund, you keep Founder OS, you keep the templates. There's no scenario where saying yes today loses you anything."

---

### O-3. "$1,997 is too much / I don't have the money."

> "I hear you. Let me ask — what would have to be true financially for this to be a yes for you?"

(Surface the real number.)

> "Because here's the math. $1,997 over 12 months is $4 a day. If your business doesn't generate $4 of new value a day from one AI employee in a year — I'd actually want to know about it. Because that's a deeper conversation than this cohort can solve. But the cohort exists to make sure that doesn't happen."

> "And if it's a cash-flow issue — we have a 3-pay option. $549 today, $549 in 30 days, $549 in 60 days. That's 18 dollars a day for the first 90 days, then nothing."

(Confirm with crew that 3-pay exists before the event.)

---

### O-4. "I can already do this with ChatGPT for $20/month."

> "You absolutely can. ChatGPT is the engine. The cohort is the chassis. ChatGPT alone is a $20-a-month tool that the average user gets $40 of value from. The cohort is the system that turns that same $20 tool into a deployed AI employee saving you 10 hours a week. Same engine. Different result."

> "Honest question — if you could already build an AI employee that's saving you 10 hours a week with $20/month, would you have come to this event?"

(They almost always laugh and concede.)

---

### O-5. "I'm not technical."

> "Perfect. That's the entire reason this exists. The whole cohort is built around — and explicitly NAMED — the principle that the only skill required is clarity. Not coding. Not technical. Clarity."

> "Stand up if you saw the demo on stage today. [pause] The volunteer wasn't technical. They pressed one button. That's the cohort. We're going to do that with YOU, three weekends in a row, until you have your own."

---

### O-6. "I'll do it myself with [free YouTube content / a course I already bought]."

> "That's a real option. Some people do. And some of those people are still trying to figure it out two years later. Here's the question — what's the COST of doing it yourself? Six months of evenings? A year of false starts?"

> "$1,997 buys you 19 hours of my time, 20 templates I've already built, and a community of people doing it with you. The cost-of-time math always wins for people who actually want this done."

(Cardone cost-of-inaction frame.)

---

### O-7. "Will this work for my industry / country / size?"

> "Two answers. One — yes. Because the AI employee blueprint isn't industry-specific. The brief / voice / output structure works for restaurants, agencies, e-commerce, real estate, doctors. We've had every type in past cohorts."

> "Two — if you specifically want to talk through your case, I want you to grab one of the team at the back of the room and talk through it before you sign up. We don't want anyone in the cohort who shouldn't be."

(The "talk to the team" move filters out the pure stallers — real fits actually go talk to the team and convert.)

---

### O-8. "What if I miss a session?"

> "Every session is recorded and you have lifetime access. You can also re-attend any future cohort. So if you miss June 21, you can sit in on the Sept 21 cohort's same session. You're never going to be out."

---

### O-9. "I just bought another course."

> "Then you already know the difference between buying a course and getting a result. The reason this works is because it's not a course. It's a cohort. Live. With me. With other founders. With templates that deploy. Courses sit on shelves. Cohorts ship product."

---

### O-10. "I'm too busy right now / I don't have the time."

> "I appreciate that. Let me reframe — the entire promise of this cohort is to GIVE YOU back time. The cost of saying no isn't just $1,997 you don't spend. It's also 6 more months of doing the work the AI was supposed to do. What's that worth to you?"

> "And it's only 19 hours over 4 weeks. Less than 5 hours a week. If you can't carve out 5 hours a week to permanently get back 10 hours a week — we have a deeper conversation to have."

---

### O-11. "AI is a fad / hype / it'll be obsolete in 6 months."

> "Maybe. Some of the technology will change. The principle won't. The principle is: stop hiring humans for tasks an AI can do, and put your humans on the things only humans can do. Whatever AI tools exist in 6 months, that principle still wins. The cohort teaches the principle, not just the tool of the day."

---

### O-12. "Can I get back to you tomorrow / next week?"

(This is a soft no with a smile.)

> "I respect that. Two things — first, the Founder OS access is room-only. After this event, even if you say yes tomorrow, you don't get that piece. So just be aware of what you're choosing between."

> "Second — and I say this kindly — Day 1, hour 1, we agreed not to leave decisions to tomorrow. Some of you in this room have made a habit of saying 'tomorrow' for the last 5 years. I'm not going to push you. But I will ask you — what are you actually going to do differently tomorrow that you can't do right now?"

(Cardone's "rather have a no" mechanic. Let the silence sit. Either they sign up or you let them go warm — they often come back during the break.)

# Crew posture during the close

While Danny is on stage, the crew at the back of the room is:

1. **Visible.** Standing. Smiling. Clipboards out. Order forms ready.
2. **Quiet during the silences.** Especially the 5-second hold on the price reveal. No movement.
3. **Active during "I'M IN."** The moment the room starts to stand and walk back, the crew opens up. Order forms move. QR codes are visible. Payment terminal is visible.
4. **Patient with stallers.** Anyone who walks back but isn't ready — talk it through. Don't rush. Don't push. Use the objection bank. The hard close is Danny's; the team's role is helpful, not pushy.

---

# Final note to Danny

The close is choreography. The room is rehearsed (by you, over two days) to do exactly what you ask. By the time the price hits the slide, the audience has already declared three times that they're the kind of person who buys this. The close isn't where you sell — it's where you give them permission to do what they've already decided.

If the room doesn't move, it's not because the price is wrong or the offer is bad. It's because the rehearsal didn't take. Diagnose backward: did you fire the anchor 4 times? Did you do the Eker declaration with full body? Did the live demos land? If any of those slipped, the close was always going to be soft. The close is the verdict on the previous 3.5 hours, not the 22 minutes.

So protect the 3.5 hours. The 22 minutes will take care of itself.
