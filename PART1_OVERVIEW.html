Part 1 · The Strategy

Why 3 Days Beats a Single-Shot Pitch

A 1-day pitch forces the room to decide before they trust you. A 3-day arc lets the room prime itself.

Day 1 — Pure value, zero sales pressure

They like you. They tell their friends at lunch about your demo. The room primes itself. No CTA, no order forms, no pressure. Open the stack at the end. Leave them leaning forward.

Day 2 last slot — The full pitch

By the time you're up, they've sat through Brunson-style pitches from 4-5 other speakers. You're the one they came for. The close lands soft because the trust is already there.

Day 3 breakout — Second touch

Fence-sitters from Day 2 (everyone who said "let me think about it") get a second touch. Buyers get deepening. Both groups leave warm for Day 4.

Day 4 repeat — Last shot

Mac bundles. Anyone who walked Day 2/3 gets a bigger frame. Three rooms to convert. Not one.

The Audience You're Selling To

This room is a hybrid: GBI veterans (operators, want tactical implementation, run on math) plus MMI graduates (already conditioned to declarations and identity-shifts).

Audience trait What it means for your design
MMI conditioned Use Eker mechanics — declarations, identity locks, "rich vs. poor" reframes
GBI tactical Show ROI math. Hard numbers. "Pays for itself in 15 days."
Asia-mixed Mandarin track in cohort. Local case studies (Singapore / KL / Jakarta)
Multi-speaker fatigue Show layer is non-negotiable. State engineering at every block
Pre-buying conditioned Already familiar with stage closes — your close needs MORE differentiation, not less
★ The Single Most Important Sentence

By the time the $1,997 number hits the slide on Day 2, the audience has declared three times out loud that they're the kind of person who builds systems that work for them. Saying no becomes inconsistent with who they've publicly been for two days.

That is the whole game. Everything else is choreography.

Part 1 · Cross-Cutting Elements

The Hooks That Run Across All 3 Days

These are the show layer. Plant Day 1 minute 12. Fire across all 3 days. By Day 3 they're tribal.

The Streisand Hook · Mission: Impossible Theme

30-second clip plays before every demo, every time, identical. By Day 3, the room cheers at the first four notes. They leave humming it. They hum it Tuesday morning when they sit down to build their first AI. That's the trigger that makes them buy.

The Anchor Word · "Tuesday"

Plant in Day 1, minute 12. "Last Tuesday I deployed three AI employees in 90 minutes." Drop it every time you describe the post-cohort future. By Day 3, just saying "Tuesday" lands a moment.

The Catchphrase · "Who Builds The AI?" → "I DO!"

Plant in Day 1, minute 14. Trigger every transition. 12+ times across 3 days. By Day 3 it's tribal — the room shouts before you even ask.

You: "Who builds the AI?"
Room: "I DO!"
You: "Louder. WHO BUILDS THE AI?"
Room: "I DO!"

The Physical Anchor

Open hand to chest. Fire on every "yes" moment. By Day 2's close, the gesture alone moves the room. Eker mechanic at industrial scale.

The Wealth Reframe (MMI Hook)

Poor people hire. Rich people deploy.

Fire on Day 1 minute 20, Day 2 minute 5, Day 2 close, Day 3 open. The MMI veterans in the room (Eker conditioning) react. The line plants the offer's worldview without selling.

State Engineering Rules

  • Stand every 15-20 minutes. Non-negotiable for an Asia 4-hour audience.
  • Power breath × 5 at every block opener. Resets the room in 30 seconds.
  • Pattern interrupt every 12 minutes. Sound, movement, callback, music — never teach >12 min flat.
  • Pick 3-5 barometer audience members in first 5 min of Day 1. Read by them.
  • Fire physical anchor on every peak yes-moment. By Day 2's close, the gesture itself triggers the state.

Activity Density

One activity every 7-10 minutes minimum. Never teach > 12 minutes in a flat block. The fix for "you teach too much" isn't less content — it's more changes.

Pre-Stage Routine (Universal · 60 min before every slot)

  1. 2 min power breathing — short fast nasal inhale, forceful mouth exhale, ~30 reps
  2. Power pose × 60s — V-shape arms, alone in green room or empty corner
  3. Recite opening 60 seconds out loud — not in your head. Out loud.
  4. Entrance anthem on earbud — same song every time, low volume
  5. Fire physical anchor at peak energy. Lock the state in.
  6. Final ritual line — yours: "I'm here for them."
Tech Contingency — Read Daily

1. Backup recording of every demo, ONE keystroke away. AV operator's only job during demos.

2. Cellular hotspot on standby. Personal hotspot, fully charged, as WiFi failover. Test Tuesday morning.

3. Failure recovery scripts taped to lectern. Lines for: WiFi dies, volunteer freezes, AI generates weird output, joke flops, you forget your place, full stack down, heckler.
